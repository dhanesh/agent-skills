# Diagnostics — triage wording, trigger signatures, anti-triggers

Load this at step 1 of the workflow.

## Triage questions (ask these, in this order)

Batch them. A serial interview is itself a stalling device.

1. **"If this turns out wrong, what does undoing it cost — in money, time, and credibility?"**
   Not *can* it be undone; what does the undoing cost. People routinely treat two-way doors as
   one-way doors, and the reversal cost is usually the fastest way to show them.
2. **"Who decides? And who genuinely has a veto, on what specific grounds?"**
   One name for the first. For the second, a veto that isn't bounded to specific grounds is
   not a veto, it's a delay.
3. **"Is there any evidence that would change your answer? What would it be?"**
   If the honest answer is "nothing", this is a values decision wearing an analysis costume.
   Say so; the user is choosing between things they want, and no protocol resolves that.
4. **"Would someone inheriting your position today make this same choice?"**
   The sunk-cost probe. If not, prior investment is doing the arguing.

## The five triggers

| Trigger | What you'll hear | Emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| **Choice overload** | Five or more live options; comparisons that loop; "I keep going round in circles" | Constraint enumeration and the vanishing-options test. Cut to three finalists on dealbreakers before any deep evaluation. |
| **Perfectionism / maximizing** | "I want to be sure it's the best"; endless further research | The satisficing bar and the deadline. More information past a point raises anxiety without raising quality. |
| **Regret avoidance** | "What if I'm wrong"; visible fear of the downside | Reversal cost, staged commitment, tripwires. If still stuck, 10/10/10: how will this feel in ten minutes, ten months, ten years? |
| **Low confidence** | "I've never done this before"; deference to whoever spoke last | The outside view. Base rates and borrowed criteria substitute for experience the user doesn't have. |
| **Ambiguous ownership** | "I need to check with —" repeatedly; no name attached to the call | Name the decider and bound the vetoes. No analytic method fixes this one. |

More than one can be live. Where they conflict, treat ambiguous ownership first — the others
cannot be resolved by someone who isn't actually the decider.

## Anti-triggers — when to stay out of the way

Do not open the protocol when:

- **The decision is made and is being narrated.** Past tense, "I've decided", "we're going
  with", "I went with". Reopening it manufactures doubt.
- **The user wants information, not a process.** "What are the tradeoffs between X and Y" is a
  request for analysis. Answer it. Offer triage only if they then get stuck.
- **The stakes are trivial and the user is being light about it.** Lunch does not need a
  score matrix.
- **Someone is in distress, or the decision concerns their safety or a relationship in
  crisis.** Step out of the protocol entirely and respond to the person.
- **A protocol is already running in this conversation.** Finish it rather than restarting.

## Detection wording, if auto-offering

One line, then stop and wait:

> This looks like a decision point — want me to run triage on it? About a minute.

The offer is the intervention. Naming the loop out loud interrupts it more reliably than
reasoning inside it, and an offer the user declines costs almost nothing — whereas a protocol
that launches uninvited is how the skill gets turned off.

## A note on how much process is warranted

Process scales with the reversal cost, not with how anxious the decision feels. Anxiety is a
signal about the person's trigger, not about the decision's stakes. A high-anxiety, low-reversal-
cost decision is a fast-path decision with a reassurance problem, and treating it as a
full-path decision confirms the anxiety rather than resolving it.
